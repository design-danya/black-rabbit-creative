## NEW — WHAT MAKES A BEER LABEL WORK

*Place this directly under the hero. This is the section that earns the page: real expertise, stated plainly. It's also the most quotable block on the page for AI assistants answering "how do I design a beer label."*

**Eyebrow:** The Craft
**H2:** Shelf presence is a design problem, not a decoration problem.

A cooler door is a wall of noise. Forty labels, all shouting, most of them using the same three moves — a mountain, a hop cone, a distressed serif. The ones that sell are the ones a person can find again.

That means designing for three distances at once:

**Across the room.** Silhouette and color block. Before anyone reads a word, they should recognize the can. If your label only works held in the hand, it doesn't work.

**At arm's length.** Hierarchy. Brand, beer name, style, ABV — in that order, without a fight between them. Most craft labels fail here: everything is the same weight, so nothing leads.

**In the hand.** Craft and detail. This is where illustration earns its place and where a drinker decides whether the beer feels worth the price.

Then the constraints that aren't aesthetic at all: TTB requirements for the government warning, alcohol content and net contents. Ink limits and substrate behavior — a color that sings on screen can die on a matte shrink sleeve. Distributor and retailer expectations for how a series reads together on a shelf.

We design against all of it at once, because the label doesn't get a second version.

## NEW — WHAT A BREWERY BRAND ACTUALLY INCLUDES

*Replaces the current flat 7-item checklist. Same items, but each earns its line — better for search, much better for a founder trying to work out what they're buying.*

**Eyebrow:** Scope
**H2:** More than a logo. A system your staff can actually run.

**Core identity** — Primary mark, secondary marks, and the variations you'll actually need: a tap handle lockup, a can top, a single-color version for embroidery, something that survives being one inch wide on a coaster.

**Can & bottle label systems** — Not one label. A structure that lets a core lineup, a seasonal, and a one-off release all look like the same brewery while staying distinct on the shelf.

**Custom illustration** — Where a brand needs something no one else can use. The Granite Roots *Cheshire Czech* label was illustrated from reference photography of the Troy train station, with Mount Monadnock anchoring the composition.

**Taproom & environmental design** — Signage, menu boards, wayfinding, wall graphics. The brand in the room where people actually decide they like you.

**Merchandise** — Shirts, glassware, coasters, hats. Designed as part of the system rather than bolted on, so a hoodie doesn't need its own logo.

**Brand guidelines** — Written for a brewery team, not a design department. Elm City's runs 31 pages and covers what a bartender or a printer needs to get right without calling us.

**Seasonal & limited-release design** — A framework for the releases you haven't brewed yet, so the tenth label is as fast to produce as the first.

## NEW — FAQ

**H2:** Brewery branding questions

**Do you handle TTB label compliance?**
We design to TTB requirements — government warning, alcohol content, net contents, and the placement rules that go with them — and supply print-ready files built to those specs. Final COLA submission stays with the brewery, since it's tied to your permit, but you won't get a label back that can't be approved.

**Can you work with our existing printer?**
Yes, and we'd rather. We'll talk to them before the design is final about substrate, ink limits, and any white-ink or varnish steps, so the file we hand over is built for that press instead of adjusted after the fact.

**We only need one label, not a whole brand. Is that a project?**
Yes. Granite Roots came to us for a single can. If the brewery's identity already works, a one-off label is a clean, contained project — and often the start of a longer conversation.

**How long does a brewery brand take?**
A single label runs a few weeks depending on illustration. A full identity — marks, label system, guidelines, merch, taproom — is a longer engagement measured in months. We scope it properly before you commit.

**Do you work with breweries outside New Hampshire?**
Yes. We're based in Portsmouth and work with breweries across New England, and remotely beyond it. Most of the work happens in files and video calls either way.

**What if we already have a logo we like?**
Then we keep it. Elm City's project was explicitly a refine-and-strengthen, not a start-over. Throwing out brand equity a brewery has already built is usually the wrong move.
