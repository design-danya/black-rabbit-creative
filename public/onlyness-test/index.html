The Onlyness
Test

Six questions build one sentence — the thing only you can say. The rule is simple: if a competitor could say it too, it isn't done yet. Answer all six, then polish it into one clean statement.

Answer in plain words · Short is stronger

W · 1

What are you?

Name your category in the plainest words possible. This is the noun a stranger would use.

e.g. a branding studio

Prompts to find your answer
  • If someone at a party asked "what do you do?", what's the plainest answer?
  • What shelf or category would a customer look under to find you?
  • Strip away every adjective — what's the bare noun that's left?

More examples: a cold-brew coffee company · a running-shoe brand · a bookkeeping service

W · 2

How are you different?

The one thing rivals can't claim. Not a list — the single sharpest difference. This is where the test is won or lost.

e.g. refuses to make generic work

Prompts to find your answer
  • Finish this: "Unlike everyone else in my category, we ___."
  • What do you refuse to do that your competitors happily do?
  • If a rival copied your look tomorrow, what still couldn't they copy?

More examples: roasts every batch the day it ships · is built for feet standard sizes ignore

W · 3

Who is it for?

Your specific customer. The narrower you go, the sharper the position.

e.g. product founders ready to invest in brand

Prompts to find your answer
  • Picture one real customer — who are they, exactly?
  • Who is this not for? Naming that sharpens the who.
  • What do your best clients have in common with each other?

More examples: chefs who care where the beans come from · marathoners with wide feet

W · 4

Where do you play?

Your market, place, or channel — the arena you compete in.

e.g. the U.S. craft & product space

Prompts to find your answer
  • What market, region, or channel is your home turf?
  • Online, in person, wholesale, retail — where do people meet you?
  • Where do your competitors not operate?

More examples: specialty grocery and direct-to-consumer · independent run shops nationwide

W · 5

Why does it matter?

Why should they care — and what's at stake if they don't?

e.g. they'd rather stand apart than blend in

Prompts to find your answer
  • What changes for the customer the moment they choose you?
  • What's the real cost of settling for the generic option?
  • Why do your clients say they came to you in the first place?

More examples: flavor dies within days of roasting · the wrong shoe ends a training season

W · 6

Why now?

The shift that makes this the moment for you to exist.

e.g. as markets get louder, sameness gets punished

Prompts to find your answer
  • What has shifted in your market recently?
  • Why would this have been harder to say five years ago?
  • What trend or tension makes you timely right now?

More examples: as buyers turn on mass-market coffee · as running booms and gear stays one-size-fits-all
